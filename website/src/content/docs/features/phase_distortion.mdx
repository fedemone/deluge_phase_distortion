Two oscillator types for synths and kit rows: `PHASE DIST`, the phase distortion of Casio's CZ series, and `VPS`,
vector phaseshaping, a modern superset of it.

## Description

A CZ oscillator reads a cosine through a bent phase. The phase climbs from 0 to 1 once per cycle; the waveform picks
how the climb is bent, and DCW how far. With DCW at 0 the five phase-bending waves are a pure sine; raising DCW adds
harmonics while the pitch stays put. On the CZ, DCW has its own envelope, which is why it sweeps like a filter
without having one.

On the Deluge, DCW is the oscillator's wave index, shown as `DCW` for these two types. Patch an envelope, an LFO or
velocity to it the same way as to a wavetable position.

The waveforms follow a real CZ-1, measured from the captures in Michael Rickard's
[CZ Spelunking](https://www.kasploosh.com/cz/11800-spelunking/) study: the edges, holds and dips of each wave, where
each wave's cycle starts, the resonant windows, and how wave combine glues two cycles together. Two things are not
known from the hardware: how DCW steps between its ends (here the bend speeds up evenly on a log scale), and whether
the double sine is a plain sine at DCW 0 (here it is). Open-source ports were consulted only as references.

Vector phaseshaping is from J. Kleimola, V. Lazzarini, J. Timoney and V. Välimäki, "Vector Phaseshaping Synthesis",
DAFx 2011.

## Usage

Set an oscillator's `TYPE` to `PHASE DIST` or `VPS`. Both work in subtractive and ring-mod modes, with unison and
oscillator sync.

### PHASE DIST

- `CZ WAVE` picks the CZ-101's waveform:
  - `SAW`: a fast edge and a slow ramp, brightening towards a sawtooth.
  - `SQUARE`: holds on the peaks; odd harmonics only.
  - `PULSE`: one narrow dip per cycle, thinning towards a pulse.
  - `DOUBLE SINE`: a sine with a narrow extra dip at its peak.
  - `SAW PULSE`: a saw that parks on its peak for part of the cycle.
  - `RESO SAW`, `RESO TRIANGLE`, `RESO TRAPEZOID`: a resonant peak that sweeps from 1 to 15 times the note under
    a falling, a rise-and-fall or a held-then-falling window, like a resonant filter on the window's own shape.
- `COMBINE` is the CZ's wave combine: the oscillator alternates between its CZ wave and this one on every cycle, which
  also adds a sub-octave. Only the five phase-bending waves combine, as on the CZ.
- `DCW` (the wave index) sets how far the wave is bent.

### VPS

- `DCW` raises the inflection point, packing extra cycles into the first part of the waveform: a formant that rises
  with it.
- `PULSE WIDTH` moves the inflection from the middle of the cycle (0) towards its start (50).

### A CZ-style patch

The CZ has three envelopes per line, for pitch (DCO), waveform (DCW) and level (DCA). One way to lay them out:

- Envelope 1 is the level, as always.
- Patch envelope 2 to oscillator 1's `DCW` and envelope 3 to oscillator 2's.
- Use oscillator 2 as the CZ's second line: the same wave slightly detuned for `1+1'`, another wave for `1+2'`, or
  ring-mod mode.
- A small negative `NOTE` cable to `DCW` works like the CZ's DCW key follow.

## Notes

- At high notes the bend is held under what the sample rate can carry, so the top of the keyboard stays clean rather
  than folding into inharmonic tones.
- Presets store the types as `phaseDist` and `vps`, with the CZ wave and combine as `pdWave` and `pdCombine`.
  Older firmware loads these oscillators as triangles.
